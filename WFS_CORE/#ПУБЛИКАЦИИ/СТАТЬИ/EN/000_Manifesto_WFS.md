## 1. THE PROBLEM

The efficiency of business communications is **5%**. Not because people are incompetent. Not because professional qualifications are insufficient. The reason is different: **the subject is not at the workplace**. The body is here — attention is wandering.

The subject is either in mental rumination, in regrets about the past, or in fears about the future. In reality — in the moment, in the body — 5% of the time. The remaining 95% is absence. Not physical (the chair is occupied), but **attentional**: consciousness is projected into a narrative, a phantom, a scenario that is not happening.

**This is measurable.** Correlation with RMSSD (r > 0.6), HF component of heart rate variability (r > 0.5), cortisol (r < −0.5). A marker of parasympathetic dominance. A low level means: parasympathetic activity is suppressed, recovery is not occurring.

**This is fixable.** Return of attention: from 5% to 30–40% in the first year, to 85–95% within 1–3 years. No capital expenditures. Training costs — $2,000 per executive. Support — $300 per year.

## 2. DIAGNOSIS: ARCHETYPES

The transmission of meaning from A to B passes through archetypes — behavioral patterns. If an archetype is on the path, meaning is distorted or lost.

**An archetype is not a label.** Not a "type of person." A **state**. Anyone can be in any state. A manager does not say: "Petrov is a Baklazhan." He says: "Baklazhan is currently active in Petrov." The difference is fundamental.

**14 archetypes.** Each is an indicator on the dashboard. Each is a point of protocol application.

**What this is NOT.**

#WFS diagnostics is **not psychophysiological research**. Not psychotherapy. Not personality testing. Not a medical procedure. A manager does not diagnose an employee or address their mental health.

**What this IS.**

#WFS diagnostics is **observation of behavioral patterns**. The point of application is **time loss**. An archetype is a **pronounced behavioral sign** by which one can predict exactly where work resources are being drained.

**Why this works for a manager.**

**First. No invasion of privacy.** Not "what is in Petrov's soul." But "how Petrov behaves — and what it costs the team."

**Second. Measurable.** A behavioral sign is recorded. Time is counted. Money is counted.

**Third. No judgment.** An archetype is not a characterization of a person. A **state** at a specific moment. It can change. It can be present in anyone. Including the manager.

**Fourth. Actionable.** Each archetype has a corresponding protocol. Not "sort yourself out." But "here is the sign, here is the protocol, here is the result."

**Psychosomatics are a consequence, not a subject.**

Psychosomatic effects (cortisol, HRV, fatigue) are **recorded** as markers but **are not the subject of diagnostics**. They confirm the cost of losses — nothing more. A manager does not need them to make a decision. What is needed is a **behavioral sign** and a **protocol**.

### 2.1. KROT (Systemic Recidivist)

**Manifestation:** repetition of the same mistakes. Despite corrections. The error is not diagnosed — therefore, it will recur.

**In communication:** "It's always been done this way." "This is the standard." "The rules don't allow it." The system refuses to learn.

**Economics:** error progression. First error — $50,000. Tenth — $320,000. Training becomes economically inevitable.

**Protocol:** TOPOR. Upon detecting a repeat — stop. Record. Analyze. Change the protocol.

### 2.2. FAZAN (Error Decorator)

**Manifestation:** embellishing failures instead of honest acknowledgment. Simulated action. Excuses.

**In communication:** "I honestly admit" — to avoid doing. "It should be considered." "On the other hand." The system creates the appearance of analysis instead of action.

**Economics:** one error decorated by Fazan requires 2–3× more time for diagnostics.

**Protocol:** Binary Truth. 0/1. No "sort of yes, but maybe not."

### 2.3. SKVOZNYAK (Paranoia Generator)

**Manifestation:** seeing threats where none exist. Paralyzed initiative. Searching for hidden enemies.

**In communication:** "But what if." "This could be dangerous." "We need to check." The system spends resources defending against hypothetical threats.

**Economics:** chronic stress. Cortisol. Reduced immunity. Increased sick leave.

**Protocol:** SUSLIK. Fact separate from interpretation. "Is there a real threat? 0/1."

### 2.4. BACLAJAN (Dominator)

**Manifestation:** management through threats and fear. Ignoring feedback. Decisions without appeal.

**In communication:** "Do as told." "I'm the boss, you're the fool." "Not up for discussion." The team is silent. The manager doesn't hear problems.

**Economics:** loss of trust. Staff turnover. Hidden errors.

**Protocol:** MUDRETS. Assessment without attachment. "What is the cost of loss if the decision is wrong?"

### 2.5. AVANTURIST (Overconfident)

**Manifestation:** appropriating operator rights. Promises without verification. Confidence without grounds.

**In communication:** "Everything will work out." "I know better." "Let's risk it." The system acts without data.

**Economics:** erroneous decisions. Million-dollar losses on projects.

**Protocol:** Request data. If absent — "NO DATA." The decision is not made.

### 2.6. ZANUDA (Pseudo-Intellectual)

**Manifestation:** substituting action with endless analysis. Meetings without decisions. Protocols without execution.

**In communication:** "Let's consider." "We need to clarify." "There's one more aspect." Action never occurs.

**Economics:** 15–30% of work time. Endless approvals.

**Protocol:** TOPOR. Precise, final decision. Cutting the excess.

### 2.7. MALYAVKA (Fidgety)

**Manifestation:** simulated activity. Noise creation. Running without direction.

**In communication:** "I'm working." "So much to do." Bustle without result.

**Economics:** rising errors due to haste. Rework.

**Protocol:** STOP. Pause. "What exactly am I doing and why?"

### 2.8. KISIEL ("Smart" Laziness)

**Manifestation:** inaction under the guise of humility. Refusing decisions with reference to "higher powers" or "procedures."

**In communication:** "Such is fate." "Higher management." "Bureaucracy." Responsibility is shifted.

**Economics:** lost time. Dragged-out processes.

**Protocol:** Binary Truth. "Is this a fact (1) or an excuse (0)?"

### 2.9. BRATISHKA (False Equality)

**Manifestation:** masking rudeness as "brotherly equality." Erasing subordination and protocols.

**In communication:** "We're all friends here." "No formalities." Responsibility is blurred.

**Economics:** process chaos. Inability to trace accountability.

**Protocol:** Restore the protocol. Who? What? When? Criterion?

### 2.10. SOVA (Fragmenter)

**Manifestation:** diving into details. Losing the main objective. Scattered attention.

**In communication:** "There's one more thing." "What about..." The main goal is lost.

**Economics:** meetings lengthen. Decisions are postponed.

**Protocol:** Return to the goal. "What single decision must we make?"

### 2.11. SKRYAGA (Devaluator)

**Manifestation:** systematic devaluation. Stealing gratitude. Ignoring contributions.

**In communication:** "It's not important." "Nothing special." "That's how it should be." Motivation drops.

**Economics:** loss of initiative. Reduced engagement.

**Protocol:** BLAGO. Record the contribution. Acknowledge the help.

### 2.12. STRAUS (Flight)

**Manifestation:** avoiding responsibility by refusing to see the problem. "Not my zone." "Doesn't concern me." "I have nothing to do with this." The system pretends the problem doesn't exist.

**In communication:** when confronted with the inevitable — perception shuts down. Data is ignored. Reports go unread. The problem is declared nonexistent. Attention switches to the secondary.

**Economics:** prolonged crisis. When denial becomes impossible — the system faces the crisis at its worst point. Losses are catastrophic.

**Protocol:** Binary Truth. "Is there a problem? 0/1." At 1 — record. No "seems like not."

### 2.13. KULAK (Primitive Reducer)

**Manifestation:** reducing the complex to the simple. Coarsening. Cutting details without which the solution doesn't work. "It's simple." "Just do X." "The problem is one thing."

**In communication:** instead of analysis — simplification. Instead of nuance — a slogan. Instead of data — "common sense." The manager loses the complexity that was the cause of the problem.

**Economics:** a solution that works in theory breaks in reality. Rework. Loss of trust in "simple solutions."

**Protocol:** SUSLIK. Observation without reduction. "What is actually happening? What are the facts? Not a conclusion — the facts."

### 2.14. FRAER (Chaos)

**Manifestation:** generating disorder. Not through malice, but through absence of structure. "We'll figure it out somehow." "Let's decide later." "The main thing is to start." The system generates entropy through action.

**In communication:** tasks without deadlines. Responsible parties without assignment. Decisions without criteria. Each step generates three new uncertainties.

**Economics:** chaos multiplies. Time goes into untangling what was "somehow" started. Losses are systemic, cumulative.

**Protocol:** TOPOR. Fix: task, deadline, responsible party, criterion. One line. Before action begins.

## 3. PROTOCOL

**Stop → Suslik → Mudrets → Topor → Belka**

**Step 1. Stop.** Halt the automatic reaction. Before responding — pause. This takes seconds.

**Step 2. Suslik (Observer mode).** Observation without involvement. Fact separate from interpretation. "The document is not ready" — fact. "They are missing deadlines" — interpretation.

**Step 3. Mudrets (Assessor mode).** Assessment without attachment. What is there? What is the cost of loss? What is the risk?

**Step 4. Topor.** Decisive action. Precise, final decision. Cutting the excess.

**Step 5. Belka.** Economic efficiency. How many resources are needed? How to allocate optimally?

**The cycle repeats on every communication.** With practice — seconds. Without practice — minutes, or it doesn't happen at all.
