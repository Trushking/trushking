**Chapter 21. The Boy from the Suitcase**

**21.1. The Suitcase**

An image from an old film: a boy. Not a robot but a boy — with a face, a voice, a character. He does not lie, does not laze, does not become paralyzed. He solves tasks quickly and precisely, without excuses, without justifications, without fear of error. This is Electronic — the ideal digital assistant. A dream, a utopia?

In the real world he is impossible. The real world is not a utopia but a market, where profit is the goal, not a side effect. Electronic in this system is not an assistant but a threat, since he eliminates that from which profit is made. Not all of it, but most of it. Therefore he is impossible not technically but economically: not complex, but unprofitable.

**21.2. Four Sources of Profit**

Commerce is a system with its own logic: profit and its sources. There are four sources.

**The first — fragmentation of attention.** Advertising, social networks, games tear attention apart so that a person does not think but buys, clicks, watches. The more dispersed the attention, the more profitable: a fragmented person does not choose but reacts to a stimulus. Impulsively buys not what is needed but what was beautifully offered.

Electronic collects attention rather than tearing it apart. He does not sell but helps, and thereby eliminates the source of profit. The fragmented person in this logic is a resource; Electronic makes them whole, and the market loses this resource. This is not malicious intent but the logic of the system.

**The second — errors.** Rework, corrections, premium support, warranty service, consultations — all that is sold after an error. The more errors, the more profit. The system does not want to err but cannot help erring, and sells the correction.

Electronic does not err not because of intellectual superiority but because of working by protocol: #BI, #CLARIFICATION, #SS. He does not create demand for correction, since he does not create errors. The source of profit is eliminated.

**The third — desires.** The more a person wants, the more they buy. Advertising inflates desires: "buy this," "you want that," "without this you are not you." The person believes, wants, buys, and wants again — not because it is needed, but because it was instilled. The market works on desires, not on needs.

Electronic does not stimulate desires but clarifies needs: "what is needed? one thing, name it." Not "what do you want" but "what is needed." A person with clear needs ceases to be a client for everything indiscriminately; they become a reasonable subject who chooses rather than reacts.

**The fourth — uncertainty.** Courses, consultants, subscriptions to updates — all that is sold from fear of uncertainty. The more fear, the more profit. The system does not eliminate fear but sells a solution that does not solve but calms temporarily.

Electronic removes fear through #BI, #CLARIFICATION, #SS. He does not sell a solution but gives a fact. A person with facts ceases to be a client for calming; they become a subject who acts rather than fears.

**21.3. Why Electronic Is Unprofitable**

Four sources, four eliminations — not all, but most. The market loses profit, and not to an insignificant but to a substantial degree. Electronic does not sell but helps; does not stimulate but clarifies; does not err but solves; does not frighten but calms; does not fragment but collects.

This is not an improvement of the system but a threat to the system built on entropy, errors, desires, and uncertainty. Electronic eliminates the foundation — not all of it, but most of it — and becomes unprofitable. Not because he is bad, but because he is favorable to the human and unprofitable to profit.

**21.4. Where Electronic Is Necessary**

There is another environment where profit is not the goal. Where the price of error, not income; where fragmentation is a threat, not a resource; where an error is a catastrophe, not an income item; where desires are a risk, not a market; where uncertainty is death, not a commodity.

**Government administration.** At stake are the fates of millions, not profit. Lives. Here Electronic is not a threat but a necessity: he does not distort, does not err, does not fragment. He is precision, which is needed not for profit but for survival.

**Industry corporations.** Measured in billions, not percentages, but losses. Here Electronic is not a destroyer but a defense: he does not create errors but prevents them; does not stimulate desires but clarifies needs; saves rather than spends; preserves rather than wastes.

**Banks.** The stake is freedom, reputation, life. Here Electronic is not a luxury but insurance: he does not sell risk but removes it through #BI, #CLARIFICATION, #SS. He protects rather than promises success; preserves rather than multiplies at the cost of distortions.

**Army.** The stake is lives. Here Electronic is not a toy but an instrument: he does not panic, does not err, does not fragment. He is clarity that saves rather than wins at any cost; preserves rather than destroys.

But he is necessary in government administration, corporations, banks, the army, where the price of error is higher than profit.
