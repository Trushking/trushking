**Chapter 19. Environment of Decision Execution: Entropy of the Collective**

**19.1. Statement**

A leader makes a decision for a collective. The decision is considered made and is transmitted into the environment for execution. The position is as follows: the quality of execution depends on two variables — the quality of the decision itself and the state of the environment. With high entropy of the environment, a decision made from clarity is executed with distortions; with low entropy — it is executed precisely. From this follows the subject of consideration: the environment of decision execution, the mechanisms that reduce the quality of execution, and the ways to eliminate them.

**19.2. The Mechanism of Distortion**

In terms of ICM the environment is described by the level of entropy H and coherence C. At C below the threshold C*(H) entropy grows: dH/dt = α(1 − C)H − βC·H² + ξ(t). The observed consequence: a decision transmitted into an environment with low coherence passes through a series of distortions. Each link of execution contributes its own share — delay, reformulation, partial execution, blocking. The total distortion manifests as a loss of quality. Estimate: at environment C of 0.15–0.3, time losses on execution grow by 30–50%, the quality of the result decreases by 1–2 points on a five-point scale. [MODEL]

**19.3. Archetypes as Sources of Entropy in the Collective**

An archetype acts on the carrier and on the environment. The mechanism of transmission is the form of speech and behavior: intonation, tempo, pauses, formulations. The receiver reads the markers and reproduces them. Below are described the mechanisms of influence on the environment for six archetypes.

**Kissel.** The carrier justifies inaction by reference to authority or will. Formulations: "the superiors know better," "there is an instruction," "do not stick out." Effect on the environment: initiative in those around is blocked; the decision is executed formally, without involvement. Mechanism: reference to authority removes responsibility from the executor and simultaneously blocks action.

**Eggplant.** The carrier forms a decision from the representation of the proper result and holds this representation when contradictory data arrive. Effect on the environment: feedback does not arrive; executors reproduce the expected, not the actual. Mechanism: pressure overlaps the feedback channel.

**Ostrich.** The carrier excludes information contradicting the current representation. Effect on the environment: problems are not noticed; the decision is executed under conditions of incomplete data. Mechanism: denial blocks the arrival of the signal.

**Bore.** The carrier continues analysis when sufficient data exist. Effect on the environment: the decision is delayed; time is spent on explaining the known. Mechanism: analysis replaces action.

**Tiny.** The carrier processes fragments of past events outside connection with the current task. Effect on the environment: chaotic activity, switching, loss of focus. Mechanism: residual data replace the current task.

**Draft.** The carrier builds a sequence of threats without support in current data. Effect on the environment: checks, approvals, delays. Mechanism: the forecast of threat blocks action until the occurrence of an event that is not established.

**19.4. Total Effect**

Each archetype in the collective lowers the coherence of the environment. When several carriers are present, the effect is amplified: carriers reproduce each other's markers, entropy grows according to the ICM formula. Estimate: a collective of 10 people with two or three carriers of active archetypes loses 20–40% of working time on distortions, rework, and approvals. [MODEL]

**19.5. Elimination**

Elimination of the entropy of the environment is performed through increasing coherence. The first mechanism — a coherent subject in the environment. When a subject with C ≥ 0.9 is present, the environment acquires a supporting structure: receivers connect as they become ready, the coherence of the environment grows, entropy decreases. The mechanism is described as biobehavioral synchronism (ICM). The second mechanism — protocols. #BI cuts off the unverifiable; #CLARIFICATION requests the missing; #SS fixes the state; #SUSLIK and #SAGE perform observation and assessment without involvement. The application of protocols reduces the level of noise and increases C. The third mechanism — fixation. An archetype noticed as a pattern loses its determining action. Fixation is performed through observation and description of the mechanism.

**19.6. Practical Consequence for the Leader**

A decision made from clarity is executed precisely in an environment with high coherence. In an environment with low coherence the decision is executed with distortions regardless of the quality of the decision. From this follows the practical conclusion: work with the decision includes work with the environment. Increasing the coherence of the environment is part of the decision-making process, not an addition to it.
