**Chapter 10. Whose Burden**

**10.1. The Load at the Crossroads**

At a crossroads stands a person with a load on their shoulders. The load is a decision. The person reflects on the method of carrying it without first determining the ownership of the load and the necessity of carrying it. Others pass by: one offers a path, another points a direction, a third silently shows. The person takes the advice offered from outside and follows the indicated direction. The basis — absence of verification. After some distance a fall occurs. The cause — not weakness, but the mismatch of the load's ownership, its content, or the direction. The key action at the crossroads is verification: "Is this my burden? And does it suit me?"

The first filter of decision concerns the ownership of the load and its correspondence. These are two questions asked before the protocols, before #BI, and before #AXE. The application of precise instruments to a load that is not the right one gives no result.

**10.2. What "Another's" Means**

Into another's zone of responsibility no invasion of attention occurs: in another's zone the subject cannot change the result, and the expenditure of psychic energy there is useless. Work is performed in one's own zone of responsibility.

"Another's" in the context of this chapter means advice, a directive, or an instruction that comes from outside into one's own zone. A leader, colleague, or specialist gives advice on a task located in the zone of responsibility of the decision-maker. A directive from management is also addressed to this zone. It is precisely such external inputs that are considered.

One's own decision is made inside the zone of responsibility: from #CoM, from available facts, from a verified state. This is one's own burden, even if heavy.

External advice that has come into one's own zone may be workable. In the absence of contradiction with inner consent — with the axiomatics, in service also with instructions and with one's own vision of the situation, which may not be visible to the leader — the advice is accepted and used. It accelerates decision-making in one's own zone.

When external advice or a directive contradicts inner consent, action does not reduce to immediate rejection or silent execution. A signal of mismatch is noticed, requiring analysis: the leader may not see what is visible on the spot; the advice itself may contain an error. Analysis is performed because the contradiction persists when ignored, which raises the entropy of the collective.

Pressure differs from advice and directive by the absence of fact and the non-admission of a question. The Eggplant acts through pressure: "Do it, or..." Stopping in this case is performed on the ground of absence of consent and fact, in the presence of force.

**10.3. The First Question: Whose Zone of Responsibility**

The first question — the ownership of the zone. Does the decision relate to the zone of responsibility of the one making it? With a negative answer action is not performed: in another's zone there will be no changes, movement there is unpromising. With a positive answer the transition to the second question is performed.

This question is accessible without inner experience and without discrimination of states. An honest answer is required: "Is this my task? Can I influence it?" The answer is accessible to everyone, including one who is not familiar with attention practices.

**10.4. The Second Check: The Body — For Those in the Mind**

The second question — the correspondence of the decision, the presence of consent. The formulation "feel the response" presupposes a tuned organ of discrimination. In a person living in the mind, this organ is not tuned: clamps, rapid breathing and pulse under anxious thoughts remain outside perception. Consciousness is in the past, future, or mental noise; the body remains without attention until pain appears.

For entry, not "feel the response" is used, but mechanical tests. Each test is performed for twenty seconds and requires no preparation. The person answers a specific question.

**Test one: breathing.** Breathing into the belly or into the chest? With breathing into the chest and shallow — the sympathetic system is active, a state of tension. With breathing into the belly and deeper — the state is closer to peace. Determined without terms and instruments.

**Second: clamps.** Three points: jaw, shoulders, belly. Clenched or relaxed? In a person in the mind the jaw is often clenched, shoulders raised, belly drawn in; this remains outside notice until a direct question.

**Third: pulse.** Does the heart beat evenly or with acceleration? Acceleration — sympathetic dominance. Evenly — closer to peace. The sensation is accessible to everyone; measurement is not required.

**Fourth: hands.** Are the palms warm or cold? Cold — constriction of vessels under stress. Warm — relaxation. The check is performed in five seconds.

**Fifth: feet.** Are the feet felt on the floor? When in the mind — they are not felt. When felt — attention is in the body, in the present.

**Reading the result.** Formulation: "there is a signal / no data." Three or more signs of clamping mean that the decision is made from sympathetic activation, that is, from the past, future, or noise. This is a signal to action: stopping, performing #CPP, returning to the check later.

**Mechanism.** The subject answers a specific question about the jaw or palms. This is accessible also to one who is not familiar with attention practices. After two or three weeks of repetition, the noticing of clamping appears without questions. This is a manifestation of consent — the result of attention to simple signals.

**Connection with metrics.** Bodily signs of clamping are the external correlate of #FI; relaxation is the correlate of #IH growth. The body check corresponds to the check of universal metrics without instruments.

**10.5. The Third Check: Consent of #CoM — For Those Who Discriminate**

For those who discriminate state, the second check proceeds differently. #CoM is the core that knows without analysis. When the decision corresponds to #CoM, consent arises — a quiet "correct," without inner dispute. It is not "I want" or "I like"; it is deeper: "This is correct, even if hard, even if frightening. Correct."

Consent does not always come immediately. Sometimes it comes through #CPP, through the clearing of noise, since noise suppresses #CoM. Archetypes speak louder, and the person hears fear, greed, or resentment, taking them for #CoM. #CPP removes the noise, and then it becomes audible — quietly, but clearly.

For the discriminating one the question is formulated: "Is there consent, silence, or dispute?" Consent — action. Silence — stopping and clarification. Dispute — stopping and analysis of the mismatch.

**10.6. Four Quadrants**

The intersection of the two questions — source and presence of consent — gives four states.

| Nature of Source | Consent Present | Consent Absent |
|---|---|---|
| **Source own** | **Norm.** Decision from #CoM, no inner conflict, #AXE works cleanly. | **Anxiety signal.** One's own, but clenched inside, no consent. Cause: archetype or #CoM not cleared. Stopping. #CPP. #CLARIFICATION. |
| **Source external** (advice, directive for one's own zone) | **Accepted burden.** Advice is workable, directive does not contradict #CoM and instructions. Execution is clean, decision is accelerated. | **Mismatch signal.** External, but no consent. Stopping. #CLARIFICATION: clarification of the mismatch. |

**First** — source own, consent present — norm: decision from #CoM, no inner conflict, action proceeds without resistance. This is the goal of the protocols, the norm to which #CPP and #BI lead.

**Second** — source external, consent present — accepted burden: advice that works; a directive that does not contradict #CoM and instructions; another's experience confirmed by fact. There is no paralysis, since there is no contradiction; the decision in one's own zone is made and accelerated.

**Third** — source own, consent absent — anxiety signal: the decision is one's own, in one's own zone, but clenched inside. Cause: archetype (Eggplant presses, Adventurer hurries, Draft frightens) or #CoM not cleared, noise drowns out consent. Stopping is performed: #CPP clears the noise, #CLARIFICATION asks the question of what stands behind the decision — fear, greed, resentment, or fact. #AXE is applied after the removal of the contradiction, through clarity.

**Fourth** — source external, consent absent — mismatch signal: advice or directive has come into one's own zone, but there is no consent. Perhaps the leader does not see the situation on the spot; perhaps the advice is outdated; perhaps there is an error in it. Action: stopping and clarification — specification, establishment of fact. The difference from the third quadrant is in the source; the reaction is one, since the signal is one: there is no consent.

**10.7. Stopping and Clarification**

The third and fourth quadrants are united by action: stopping and clarification. Stopping takes the form of a pause before action, necessary for establishing the mismatch. Rebellion, refusal, and complaint belong to other forms of action.

**Mechanism.** A decision — one's own or external — does not give consent. This means the presence of an error, a hidden mismatch, or #CoM drowned out by noise. In all cases execution without analysis entails the execution of an error, the accumulation of hidden mismatch, or the acceptance of #DE noise for a decision.

Stopping is an active request: "What exactly does not add up? Where is the fact, and where is the interpretation? What is not visible?" External advice is checked: compared with facts, with instructions, with the vision of the situation on the spot. When confirmed by fact, the advice is accepted, consent appears, the contradiction is removed. In the absence of confirmation, the advice is rejected on the basis of fact.

The stop-machine works at the first stage — before, while it is possible to correct and while it is not too late. Silent execution of what does not give consent is more expensive than stopping: the error is visible, the hidden mismatch remains invisible until the moment when correction becomes impossible.

**10.8. Connection with Protocols**

The chapter stands after the Compendium of Protocols and is connected with them as an axis.

- **#ZR** determines the boundary: no invasion into another's zone occurs, work is performed in one's own; external inputs that have come into one's own zone are considered as advice, directive, or pressure.
- **#CoM** determines the presence of consent: the core that knows, but which is not heard under the noise of entropy.
- **#CLARIFICATION** determines what stands behind the absence of consent: fact, instruction, or archetype. With fact the decision is revised; with archetype #CPP is performed. Form — a question to oneself, to others, to fact.
- **#CPP** clears the noise so that #CoM is heard: a technical operation after which consent either is or is not, and this is clear.
- **#AXE** acts in the presence of consent — in the first and second quadrants, through clarity.

**10.9. For the Leader**

The first filter is applied before all decisions, orders, and advice. Two questions, thirty seconds.

**First question:** does the decision relate to my zone? With a negative answer action is not performed: in another's zone there will be no changes. With a positive one — transition to the second question.

**Second question:** is there consent? For a person in the mind consent is checked through the body: breathing, clamps, pulse, palms, feet. For the discriminating one — through the direct consent of #CoM.

- One's own and consent present — action.
- External (advice, directive for one's own zone) and consent present — acceptance and use, acceleration of the decision.
- One's own and no consent — stopping, #CPP, analysis.
- External and no consent — stopping and clarification of the mismatch.

A decision made without contradiction is cleaner. A decision made through force costs more — not immediately, but upon subsequent discovery of the contradiction, destruction, and missed opportunities. A leader who checks themselves before a decision is stronger than one who decides quickly. Correct means clean, and cleanliness begins with the question: "Is this my zone? And is there consent?"

**10.10. Connection with Coherence**

The filter has a direct physiological correlate. The ability to discriminate the consent of #CoM and to distinguish it from the noise of fear or greed is determined by coherence (C).

With low coherence attention is fragmented, archetypes are active, and the person takes for the consent of #CoM the impulse of the Eggplant, Adventurer, or Draft. With high coherence the noise is reduced, and the consent of #CoM becomes distinguishable — quiet, but clear.

For a person in the mind, bodily tests are an external support that does not require high coherence. They allow one to see clamping even with undeveloped inner discrimination. Through repetition the tests become a habit, the habit leads to the growth of coherence: attention returns to the body, the body returns to the present, gradually the ability to discriminate state without questions appears.

The two questions — "is this my zone?" and "is there consent?" — are a check of state. A person with low coherence may pass through the quadrants formally and err, since their #CoM is drowned out. A person with high coherence hears consent without effort, since the noise does not interfere. A decision made without contradiction turns out to be exact not as a consequence of correct reflection, but as a consequence of being in a state where precision is possible.
