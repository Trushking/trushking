**Chapter 3. Exit from the Fog**

**3.1. Fog and Flashlights**

A person stands in fog. Thick, milky, not allowing sight beyond an outstretched arm. A decision must be made now, and the person takes out a flashlight: the beam snatches a piece of space, then another, another. The flashlight helps to see better but does not remove the fog, and the picture does not come together, because the fog closes behind the beam.

The arsenal of methods is arranged the same way. Each method illuminates its own direction: the Wald criterion — the worst case, the Bayesian approach — probabilities, Monte Carlo — scenarios. They help to orient in the fog but do not lead out of it. The person remains in the fog with a flashlight and with a decision they cannot make. #WFS offers not another flashlight or a powerful searchlight, but an exit from the fog — a transition to a place where fog is absent and where a decision becomes not a feat but a natural consequence of clarity.

**3.2. A Paradigm Shift, Not Another Method**

#WFS is not an addition to the arsenal and not another book on the shelf. It is a paradigm shift, a shift of optics, and a shift of question.

The arsenal asks how to manage uncertainty; #WFS asks how to eliminate it. The arsenal asks how to reduce risk; #WFS studies how to remove the cause of risk. The arsenal asks how to choose the best of the worst; #WFS investigates what actually is. The difference between these questions is fundamental: it corresponds to the difference between perfect rowing and reaching the shore.

**3.3. The Root of Uncertainty Is Inside**

Modern methods assume that uncertainty is a property of the external environment: the market is unstable, data are incomplete, forecasts are imprecise. This is only half the picture. The other half is internal uncertainty, that is, the state of the subject making the decision.

The classical question of what prevents a decision under uncertainty receives a direct answer in #WFS: what prevents it is the false ego, striving for control and fearing its loss, error, or external vulnerability. It is this fear, not the absence of data, that paralyzes action.

Modern methods try to eliminate uncertainty from the outside: collect more data, build more scenarios, calculate more probabilities. #WFS solves it from the inside, through the restoration of reason that does not fear uncertainty because it rests on #CoM. This is not psychology and not reassurance, but engineering: the root of noise is removed, and after this the methods that previously did not work begin to work — not because they changed, but because the one who applies them changed.

**3.4. The Threshold**

There is an image of a room with a door. A person stands on the threshold: not in the room and not outside, but precisely on the boundary. This is a point that is not a place in space — it is a place in state. Here the person has not yet entered but is no longer outside; here they can see both the room and themselves.

The threshold is not a metaphor but a starting point. Without it there is no decision, only wandering through a room that does not exist, through corridors that do not lead, through thoughts that are not facts. The first step consists not in entering but in standing on the threshold and seeing: not the room, but oneself. Where am I, what is known, what is not. This is not analysis but presence — here, now, without past, without future, without noise.

**3.5. Three Forces That Pull from the Threshold**

The subject cannot remain on the threshold not because they do not wish to, but because they do not see. Three forces pull them away from the threshold.

**The first — regrets about the past.** "It should have been different," "I could have," "if only then." This is not memory but hooks that pull backward, into what has already been and cannot change. The person does not think about the past — they live in it. And the threshold is here, the past is there, and the person is not on the threshold but in the past.

**The second — fears about the future.** "What if," "and if," "what will be." This is not planning but a web that entangles forward, into what has not yet happened and may not happen. The person is not preparing — they are fearing. And the threshold is here, the future is there, and the person is not on the threshold but in the future.

**The third — mental noise.** This is not reflection about the past and not anxiety about the future, but a shuffling of recent fragments: recent conversations, individual words, others' intonations, scraps of ideas, episodes of the past day. The person is not solving a task and not building a plan — they are scrolling through what has already happened: what was said, what should have been said, how it could have been perceived, who thought what.

The material of such shuffling is neither a plan nor a memory in the precise sense. It is residual data that have lost connection with the event that generated them. This state is defined as data entropy / entropic data / outdated data (#DE). The information is preserved but has lost relevance and structure: fragments are equivalent, order is absent, the significant is not separated from the secondary. The mind is occupied not with the task but with sorting such #DE, and therefore finds neither support nor exit. The person does not think — they make noise.

From this follows the third position. If a decision is made in a state of mental noise, it is made not from reality but from #DE. Reality contains facts that can be verified. Noise contains impressions that cannot be verified. A decision grown from impressions appears justified to the one who makes it and does not correspond to the state of affairs. This is not an error of thinking but a substitution of source: instead of data about the present, unverifiable material about the past is used.

Noise does not stop reflection and does not interrupt the internal dialogue. It transfers it to another material. Instead of analyzing what actually exists, the mind analyzes assumptions: who thought what, what was said wrongly, how it will be perceived. From one assumption a second is derived, from the second a third, and between them a connection is built that appears logical. The mind does not check where the first link came from. It takes it as fact and then works with derivatives.

Such a chain quickly becomes stable for the one who accepts it and does not correspond to the state of affairs. The person begins to believe in it. This belief acts as a placebo or nocebo but differs from a working placebo in its source. A working placebo rests on an inner core, on coherence, and therefore is able to hold direction. A belief grown from noise rests on uncertainty: it has no force that would hold the intention, and therefore it disintegrates at the first collision with reality. The instability of such a belief generates new noise, and noise generates new belief. Thus chaos is formed, in which erroneous decisions are made not from what is but from what is assumed.

**3.5.1. The Nature of the Mind: Not to Eliminate but to Use**

The threshold is not visible because attention is occupied with assumptions, not with facts. The three forces are not enemies but habits of the mind that hide not the room but the threshold.

Here a precise distinction is important. The mind by its nature is an instrument of reflection. It cannot work without data, just as a calculator cannot count without digits and without knowledge of the operation. This is not a defect and not a "fear of emptiness." This is a function: the mind is created for processing, and it processes what is given to it. When facts are given — it builds facts. When assumptions are given — it builds assumptions. When there are no data — it takes residual impressions, because it cannot not work.

From this follows a fundamental position. #WFS does not propose to eliminate the mind, to stop the internal dialogue, or to withdraw into emptiness. Such a path is detachment from the world, which leads to the #STOP protocol, the #SUSLIK and Ostrich archetypes, that is, to paralysis under the guise of peace. #WFS proposes something else: to give the mind correct data and correct operations. Correct data are #BI and #CLARIFICATION: fact or request, without conjecture. Correct operations are #SAGE (assessment without attachment) and #AXE (cutting off the superfluous). The mind is not switched off — it begins to work according to its purpose.

From this same follows the conclusion about the value of a coherent subject. A coherent subject is not necessarily a specialist in a specific field. But they are an excellent diagnostician: they see what is invisible to others, because others are absent in reality, and they are present. They notice substitution, distortion, discrepancy — not because they know more, but because they see what is, not what is assumed. This is viveka: discrimination of the real from the unreal. And this is a value that cannot be replaced by any volume of knowledge.

To return attention is not an effort and not a struggle, but a return. Like returning home after a long road: not cutting through the forest, but coming out onto the road that always existed, only it was not seen.

**3.8. Resistance: Archetypal Load**

Resistance to the threshold has an internal source — archetypal load: stable patterns that activate under fragmented attention and hold the subject outside the present. Below they are described as observable mechanisms, without evaluation.

The threshold is characterized by the absence of material of the past, the future, and noise. Archetypes function on this material and under the conditions of the threshold have no support. Each archetype shifts attention into its own area.

**Mole:** shift into the past. Mechanism: reproduction of previously learned schemes of action regardless of changed conditions. Formulations: "it was so," "it is accepted," "it worked before." Function: holding the subject in the area of known experience. Result: current data are not considered as a basis for action.

**Eggplant:** shift into the projection of control. Formation of a decision from the representation of the proper result and holding this representation when contradictory data arrive. Formulations: "the decision is made," "the instruction is given." Function: holding the subject in the area of assumed control. Result: feedback is not taken into account.

**Draft:** shift into the forecast of threat. Construction of a sequence of possible unfavorable outcomes without support in current data. Formulations: "what if," "and suddenly." Function: holding the subject in the area of assumed danger. Result: action is blocked until the occurrence of an event that is not established.

**Adventurer:** shift into the forecast of result. Construction of an attractive outcome without checking its foundations. Formulations: "unique offer," "there is an opportunity." Function: holding the subject in the area of the assumed result. Result: current data are replaced by expected ones.

**Tiny:** shift into residual data. Processing of fragments of past events — conversations, intonations, episodes — outside connection with the current task. Defined as data entropy (#DE): information is preserved, structure is lost. Function: holding the subject in the area of residual material. Result: the mind is occupied with sorting fragments; the task is not considered.

**General mechanism.** The five described patterns have one observable function: shifting attention from the present into the area of the past, control, threat, result, or residual data. In all cases current data cease to be the basis of decision. The patterns manifest in the form of an objection: "yes, but..." after which a return to the corresponding area follows.

**3.12. Two Approaches to Stopping the Mind**

**Approach 1: forced stopping without a point of support.**

Practice: by effort the flow of thoughts is stopped. The goal is the state of thoughtlessness. The point of support is absent: attention is not held on either fact or presence.

Characteristics of the state: the interval of thoughtlessness is short-lived (seconds, in individual cases up to a minute); the state is accompanied by discomfort; stability is absent; after the interval the mind returns to the previous mode.

Observable result with prolonged practice: the state of thoughtlessness is not consolidated; signs of pacification are not recorded; instability is preserved.

Mechanism: the effort is directed at eliminating thoughts. The data on which the mind could rely are not introduced. The interval remains empty — without content on which one could rely when the mind returns to work.

**Approach 2: stopping as a consequence of a point of support.**

Practice: first a point of support is established (#CoM). Then #SUSLIK mode is activated: observation without involvement. The stopping of reflection arises as a consequence, not as a result of effort.

Characteristics of the state: interval 1–5 seconds; clarity, brightness, distinctness of perception; fixation of reality in the form in which it is.

Function of the interval: reboot. In the interval data are fixed, which are then introduced into the work of the mind. The duration of the interval is not increased and is not a goal.

Observable result: the mind returns to work with established data. Then — assessment (#SAGE), cutting off the superfluous (#AXE), action from clarity in a permanent contour (BELKA / Electronic).

**Discrimination of mechanisms.**

| Parameter | Approach 1 | Approach 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Point of support | Absent | #CoM |
| Source of stopping | Effort | consequence of observation |
| Duration | seconds–minute, unstable | 1–5 seconds, stable |
| Content of interval | emptiness | noticed fact |
| What happens after | return to previous mode | introduction of data, assessment, action |
| Goal | thoughtlessness as a state | reboot before action |

**Position.** In approach 1 the stopping of the mind is a goal. In approach 2 the stopping of the mind is a stage. In the first case the interval remains without content. In the second case the interval is filled with a fact that is then used.
