**Chapter 18. Voice in the Receiver**

**18.1. Three Seconds**

Night, telephone, a call. Three seconds before the answer. In the receiver — breathing, then a shout: "Help! Fire! People!" This is not a message but an emotional explosion. There are no facts in it: neither address nor number of people. Only fear and time, which leaves with every second.

The dispatcher hears emotions and cannot isolate facts. The questions "where? what address? how many people?" remain unanswered because of the absence of contact with reality. The cause is not unreasonableness but that fear drowns out. Time passes, the decision is delayed, people die. Not because they did not want to, but because they did not manage to hear each other. This happens every day: thousands of calls, thousands of situations, thousands of seconds. The price is not in the report but in lives.

**18.2. Panic as an Archetype**

Panic is the Draft in extreme form. Not anxiety but horror. The person does not think, does not assess, does not choose — they react emotionally. This is not information; it is a distress signal that contains no data. The Draft paralyzes the mind. The person cannot name the address not because they do not know it, but because the mind is occupied with survival. Every thought — "I am going to die now," every word — "help." Facts disappear: they are not forgotten, they are inaccessible, because the mind does not let them through, too occupied with fear.

This is not weakness but the norm for a fragmented mind in a critical situation. And it is a problem, because facts are needed now, to save.

**18.3. The Anesthesia Protocol**

The #WFS-agent at the entrance performs the function of anesthesia. It does not calm and does not console, but removes tension and switches from panic to fact. Softly, carefully, without pressure. Five steps.

**First step — acknowledgment.** "You called — that is correct. You are already acting." The formulation contains no call to calm down or not to cry. The person has already taken a step — they called, and this is correct. Acknowledgment removes part of the tension, sufficient to hear the next question.

**Second — care.** "I hear you. I am here to help." This is not a formality but presence. The person is not alone, someone is there, and this someone will not abandon them. A support on which one can lean in reality.

**Third — a question based on fact.** "Name the address. One word — the street." Not "tell me everything," not "what happened." One question, one fact, simple and concrete. The mind catches on not the fear but the word "street," and answers.

**Fourth — confirmation.** "Accepted. You are on street X. Now I will clarify details." Confirmation fixes the fact. The person has heard, understood, and they are no longer alone. The fact exists, is fixed, and one can move on — to the next fact, calmly.

**Fifth — handover.** At the entrance the dispatcher already has not chaos but structure: address, situation, what is known and what is not. They act not from assumptions but by fact.

Time — thirty seconds, not two minutes. The five steps do not replace the dispatcher but prepare them: so that they receive information, not emotions; so that time is spent not on searching for words when receiving the call but on saving.

The firefighters left not arbitrarily but by address. Faster, more precisely, they save. The difference is not in speed but in structure. Without #WFS — emotions, with #WFS — facts. Not because the agent is smarter, but because the protocol removes tension and switches to reality. Softly, carefully, without pressure.

**18.4. Why This Works**

Panic is not unreasonableness but defense. The mind goes into a shout because it cannot cope with reality. The shout is a way to survive, instinctive and useless for salvation, because facts are needed, and emotions do not give them.

The #WFS-agent does not fight panic and does not suppress it. It wraps it, like a blanket, around the fear, and directs it — not against, but together. "You called — that is correct" instead of "stop shouting." Acknowledgment and switching. "Name the street" instead of "calm down." A simple, concrete question, so that the mind catches not on fear but on the word.

This is not manipulation and not a technique but care. The goal is not to calm but to save. For this, facts are needed, not an emotional signal. The #WFS-agent helps to name the fact — not instead of the person, but together with them. Softly, carefully, without pressure.

**18.5. Connection with Coherence and #Human_Veto**

Panic is a state with extremely low coherence (C). Attention is fragmented, #FI is maximal, access to facts is blocked. The Anesthesia protocol does not calm but increases C through a sequence of simple steps: acknowledgment, care, a question based on fact. Each step reduces #FI and returns attention to the present. By the moment of handover to the dispatcher the person is no longer in panic but in a state where facts are accessible.

This is the same mechanism as in the #WFS-wrapper for machines, only here the wrapper is human: a protocol that does not suppress panic but switches it to fact. Important: the final decision always remains with the human — the dispatcher, the firefighter, the rescuer. #Human_Veto. The machine does not decide; it prepares facts. The human decides.

**18.6. What This Gives**

Emergency services are not an example for the book but life, where every second has a price, every fact is salvation, panic is not an obstacle but death. The #WFS-protocol here is not theory but an instrument that works not someday but now, not somewhere but here.

For dispatchers, rescuers, heads of services this is not a technology but a skill. It can be mastered, implemented, and it saves — not in a report but in lives. When the protocol works, a person does not die. Not because it is an accident, but because the fact was named. In time, calmly, clearly.
