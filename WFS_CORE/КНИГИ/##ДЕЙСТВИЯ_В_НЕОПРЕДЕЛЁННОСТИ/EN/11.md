**Chapter 6. Map of the Minefield**

**6.1. The Field**

An image unfolds: a field, grass, wind, silence. The surface is perceived as even. People move across the field and make plans. Beneath the surface are mines: they are not visible, not audible, have no smell. On contact, an explosion occurs. Archetypes are arranged analogously. They are stable patterns of behavior, habitual and unnoticed. The subject moves across the field thinking they retain control; but the pattern is not in the focus of their attention. When the pattern activates, a breakdown occurs. The subject notes the result but does not note the cause: "why, if the actions were performed correctly." The cause is that the pattern was not visible. The map of the minefield performs the function of recognition. Observation: a list of patterns with a description of the mechanism. The function of the map is indication: where the pattern is, how it manifests, what it produces. On detection, the pattern becomes visible and loses its force of influence.

Result: action is performed from clarity, because the pattern is noticed.

**6.2. Sixteen Patterns**

Below are described sixteen archetypes. For each, the mechanism of time theft, paralysis, and normalization is noted.

**Mole — inertia.** Mechanism: reproduction of a previously learned scheme regardless of changed conditions. Time theft: repetition of a decision that has stopped working. Paralysis: blocking of perception of the new. Normalization: "this is experience," "this is tradition," "this is reliability."

**Ostrich — denial.** Mechanism: exclusion of information contradicting the current representation. Time theft: postponement of consideration. Paralysis: blocking of action when a signal arrives. Normalization: "this is not my business," "this cannot be."

**Sluggard — apathy.** Mechanism: absence of initiative when a task exists. Time theft: postponement without foundation. Paralysis: blocking of the first action. Normalization: "later," "not now," "no strength."

**Draft — paranoia.** Mechanism: construction of a sequence of threats without support in current data. Time theft: expenditure on checks. Paralysis: blocking of action until the occurrence of an assumed event. Normalization: "caution," "foresight."

**Eggplant — dominance.** Mechanism: formation of a decision from the representation of the proper result and holding this representation when contradictory data arrive. Time theft: expenditure on overcoming resistance. Paralysis: blocking of feedback. Normalization: "strong character," "firmness."

**Pheasant — decoration.** Mechanism: construction of an explanation after the discovery of an error. Time theft: expenditure on justification. Paralysis: blocking of acknowledgment. Normalization: "diplomacy," "flexibility."

**Tiny — chaotic activity.** Mechanism: processing of fragments of past events outside connection with the current task. Time theft: expenditure on sorting residual data and rework of many small errors. Paralysis: blocking of concentration. Normalization: "activity," "being busy."

**Miser — devaluation.** Mechanism: understatement of another's contribution and understatement of one's own result. Time theft: expenditure on recalculation. Paralysis: blocking of accounting. Normalization: "modesty," "economy."

**Bore — analysis paralysis.** Mechanism: continuation of analysis when sufficient data exist. Time theft: expenditure on additional rounds. Paralysis: blocking of decision. Normalization: "depth," "thoroughness," "foresee all variants."

**Owl — fragmentation.** Mechanism: decrease of attention under fatigue or withdrawal into insignificant details, loss of focus. Time theft: growth of errors. Paralysis: blocking of precision. Normalization: "night work," "tight schedule," "important moment," "details should be worked out."

**Adventurer — risk.** Mechanism: construction of an attractive outcome without checking foundations. Time theft: expenditure on failed projects. Paralysis: blocking of verification. Normalization: "enterprise," "courage."

**Buddy — familiarity.** Mechanism: reduction of distance and cancellation of formal protocols. Time theft: expenditure on restoring order. Paralysis: blocking of subordination. Normalization: "one of us," "simplicity."

**Fist — primitive reducer.** Mechanism: reduction of a complex task to a crude action or primitive decision. Time theft: expenditure on correcting consequences. Paralysis: blocking of strategy. Normalization: "decisiveness," "directness," "all genius is simple," "let us not complicate."

**Kissel — false humility.** Mechanism: reference to authority or will in the absence of action. Time theft: expenditure on waiting. Paralysis: blocking of initiative. Normalization: "humility," "obedience," "better not to stick out," "the superiors know better."

**Zuko — resentment.** Mechanism: retention of charge from a past event. Time theft: expenditure on scrolling. Paralysis: blocking of the present. Normalization: "justice," "principle."

**Frayer — chaos.** Mechanism: introduction of an illogical action into a debugged system. Time theft: expenditure on restoration. Paralysis: blocking of logic. Normalization: "non-standardness," "creativity."

**6.3. The Mechanism of Time Theft**

A model situation is given: a leader receives the task of launching a product by the end of the quarter. Work does not begin. Formulations are recorded: "we need to analyze the market more," "we need to coordinate with lawyers." A month passes. The deadline ends. The product is not launched.

Composition of patterns: Bore (analysis), Mole (inertia), Draft (search for threats). Each pattern contributes its share as justified. The actual loss is a quarter, accounted for in expenses as the human factor. Lost profit — in model units.

**6.4. The Cost of the Pattern**

Sequence: loss of time, rework, reputational costs, material costs, turnover, drop in quality, loss of market.

Time losses by pattern (model estimates, require a pilot): Tiny — 20%, Pheasant — 15%, Draft — 12%, Mole — 10%, Bore — 10%, Sluggard — 8%. In total up to 75% of working time. Conservative return through #WFS — 85%.

**6.5. Normalization**

Mechanism: each pattern forms an explanation in which its action looks like a positive quality. "This is experience." "This is caution." "This is activity." "This is leadership and strength." The explanation is accepted because it corresponds to the expected representation of oneself.

**6.6. The Function of the Map**

The map performs the function of recognition. A pattern detected as an archetype loses its force of influence, because its mechanism becomes visible. The purpose of the map is instruction: where the pattern is, how it manifests, what it produces. On detection, action is easily performed from clarity.

**6.7. The Mechanism of Contagion**

**Position.** The pattern acts on the carrier and on the environment. The carrier loses time; the environment receives a signal.

**Channel of transmission.** Transmission is performed through speech, intonation, tempo, pauses, and formulations. Markers of low coherence: fragmented speech, jumping between topics, contradictory instructions, multiple clarifications of the same thing, raising of tone, reduction of distance. The listed markers are fixed by the receiving side independently of content.

**Mechanism.** The receiving subject recognizes the markers of threat and shifts into defense mode. Effect: their coherence decreases, #FI grows. In a state of defense they reproduce the same markers: fragmentation, chaotic activity, defensive formulations. Transmission is carried out without intention and without content — through form.

**Resonance.** Mutual amplification is observed. Subject A with low coherence transmits markers to subject B. Subject B lowers coherence and transmits markers back. Each cycle raises the entropy of the environment. In terms of ICM this is described as mutual amplification of noise in the interaction of two low-coherence systems.

**Propagation.** One carrier transmits the signal to several receiving subjects. Each receiving subject becomes a carrier. The number of carriers grows; the entropy of the environment grows according to the ICM formula: dH/dt = α(1 − C)H − βC·H² + ξ(t). At C below the threshold C*(H) entropy grows; the threshold is reached at C ≈ 0.8–0.9.

**Observed consequences in the collective.** Growth of rework; growth in the number of approvals; decrease in the speed of decisions; growth of conflicts; growth of turnover. Each consequence is fixed as a separate loss, and the source is one: contagion.

**Economics of contagion [MODEL].** One carrier with C ≈ 0.15 in a collective of 10 people lowers the coherence of the group by 0.1–0.2 over several weeks. Time losses of the group grow by 15–25%. At a rate of $50/hour and 2,000 hours per year, the loss per group is $150,000–250,000/year.

**6.8. The Mechanism of Tuning the Environment**

**Position.** A coherent subject acts on the environment. The mechanism is the same channel: speech, intonation, tempo, pauses, formulations.

**Markers of high coherence.** Full speech; absence of contradictions, of hashed turns; absence of unnecessary clarifications; even tempo; pause before answering; precise formulations; absence of defensive reactions.

**Mechanism.** The receiving subject recognizes the absence of threat markers and remains in observation mode. Observed effect: their #FI decreases; the ability to discriminate grows. They begin to reproduce the form: full speech, pause, precision. Transmission occurs in the same way — through form.

**Resonance.** Mutual amplification is observed in the opposite direction. Subject A with high coherence transmits markers to subject B. Subject B raises coherence and transmits markers back. Each cycle lowers the entropy of the environment. In terms of ICM this is described as the coordinated dynamics of two systems passing into one state when conditions are met.

**Condition of transmission.** Transmission is performed when the operator's C is not lower than 0.9. At C below the threshold transmission does not arise: the environment does not recognize the signal as a reference. This position is fixed in ICM as qualification dependence: the result is determined by the state of the transmitter, not by the content of the message.

**Propagation.** One carrier transmits the signal to several receiving subjects. Each receiving subject becomes a carrier. The number of carriers grows; the entropy of the environment decreases. With a sufficient number of coherent links, the environment acquires a supporting structure: the rest connect as they become ready.

**Consequences in the collective.** Reduction of rework; reduction of approvals; growth in the speed of decisions; reduction of conflicts; reduction of turnover. Each consequence is a released resource.

**Economics of tuning [MODEL].** One coherent subject in a collective of 10 people raises the coherence of the group by 0.1–0.2 over several weeks. Time losses of the group decrease by 15–25%. At a rate of $50/hour and 2,000 hours per year, $150,000–250,000/year is released per group.

**6.9. Two Directions of One Parameter**

Contagion and tuning are two directions of action of one parameter: coherence C. The mechanism of transmission is one: the form of speech and behavior. The difference is in sign: at low C the entropy of the environment grows; at high C the entropy of the environment decreases.

From this follows a position for practice: a change in the environment occurs through a change in the state of the transmitter. The content of messages is secondary in relation to the state from which they are transmitted.
