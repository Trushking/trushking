**Introduction. The Point That Cannot Be Seen**

A person sits at a desk — in an office, at headquarters, in a meeting. The body's location is defined: address, position, time. All of this is subject to observation and recording.

But the decision is made at a different point, one that has no spatial coordinates. This point is the state of attention. While it remains outside the field of view, the verdict rests on a foundation that does not exist.

There is no assessment here. A law operates: distorted input produces distorted output. The law holds regardless of anyone's attitude toward it.

**Why This Book**

The science of decision-making has accumulated an extensive arsenal. The Wald criterion, the Savage criterion, Bayesian networks, Monte Carlo, multi-criteria analysis, VUCA, Cynefin, OODA. Each method is logical. Each is verified by mathematics.

In real uncertainty the arsenal falls silent: data are absent, probabilities are unknown, the decision-maker is fragmented. The methods were built for other conditions.

The book proposes a paradigm shift. Three shifts: from managing probabilities to working with facts; from reducing risk to eliminating uncertainty; from ignoring the decision-maker's state to working with it.

**Who It Is For**

Those who make decisions with a high cost of error. The stake is lives, freedom, reputation: presidents, generals, judges, corporate leaders, emergency service dispatchers.

Developers who build machines that make decisions. The task is to see the limits and construct an external contour.
