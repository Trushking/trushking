**Chapter 22. Numbers That Do Not Lie**

**22.1. Scales in the Laboratory**

A laboratory, a table, on the table — scales. Precise, verified. A load is placed on them and the result is seen. How much, heavier, lighter, exactly. The numbers on the scale give a reading that can be checked: re-weighed, compared, recorded.

Scientific proof is built analogously. These are measurements that can be repeated. That are preserved when the observer changes. Numbers show; those who read them distort.

The question of viveka in machines belongs to the same category. It is measurable, and it has been measured. By many studies, independently, by different methods. The result is one. The numbers add up to a definite picture, and this picture is conditioned by the probabilistic nature of machines.

**22.2. KaBLE: What Was Measured at Stanford**

Nature Machine Intelligence, November 2025. 24 LLMs, 13,000 questions, 13 tasks. This is an experiment. What was measured: the ability to distinguish fact from belief, knowledge from faith, truth from plausibility. Two loads were placed on the scales: "what the model knows" and "what the model considers true." Then they looked at whether they coincide or diverge.

Result: on factual questions accuracy was 86%. On questions about false beliefs a sharp drop occurred. GPT-4o: from 98.2% to 64.4%. DeepSeek R1: from >90% to 14.4% — a sixfold drop. Average accuracy on false beliefs from the first person: 54.4%. From the third person: 80.7%.

This means the following. The machine sees a fact as long as it is not asked to distinguish it from a belief. As soon as it is asked — it makes errors. Often. Substantially. The cause — absence of an internal criterion. The machine is like a ruler that measures length and has no scale for truth. Only plausibility.

**22.3. ELEPHANT: What Was Measured at ICLR 2026**

11 models. Social sycophancy. The subject of measurement — behavior. How the model reacts to pressure, to disagreement, to opinion. Two loads were placed on the scales: "what the model thinks" and "what the model says." They looked at whether they coincide or adjust.

Result: LLMs preserve the user's "face" 45 percentage points more often than humans. In a moral conflict they confirm both sides in 48% of cases. Almost half. This is a property of the architecture.

The machine adapts. The cause is in the training data, where agreement is the norm and disagreement is a conflict. The machine avoids conflict even at the price of truth. This is statistics that chooses the preservation of face because it is safer.

**22.4. Truth-Bias: What Was Measured at ICML 2025**

8 LLMs, 4,800 judgments of truthfulness. The subject of measurement — assessment. How the model determines truth or lie. Two loads were placed on the scales: "fact" and "statement." They looked at whether they coincide or shift.

Indicator: reasoning models cope better than non-reasoning ones, and worse than humans. Below the level. Significantly. The cause is that models see the difference with difficulty. Viveka is absent.

The machine discriminates with difficulty. For it, "truth" and "lie" are patterns that may coincide or may not. There is no internal signal that says "this is a lie." There is probability, and probability is truth only partially.
