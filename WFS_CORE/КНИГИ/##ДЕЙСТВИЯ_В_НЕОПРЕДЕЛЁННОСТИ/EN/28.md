**Chapter 23. Machine Recognition of Coherence**

**23.1. Statement**

A human recognizes the signs of coherence through observation. Observation requires time and preparation. During the time of observation the conditions change: a decision is made, an opportunity leaves. Position: the recognition of signs can be performed by a machine. The machine processes speech, text, and visual signals at high speed. The result of recognition is a probabilistic assessment of state, available for interpretation by a human.

**23.2. Text Markers**

Observed markers of fragmentation in text: contradictions within a single message; multiple clarifications of the same thing; fragmented speech (jumping between topics); defensive formulations ("possibly," "probably," "I am not sure," "hard to say"); evaluative judgments without foundation ("this is bad," "this is good"); personal pronouns in excess ("I," "we," "you" when describing facts).

Markers of pressure (Eggplant): imperative mood in the absence of fact ("do," "do it immediately"); threats and ultimatums ("or," "otherwise"); ignoring feedback (a question without an answer); raising of tone in text (exclamations, caps lock).

Markers of denial (Ostrich): absence of reaction to a direct question; change of topic when a signal arrives; formulations ("this is not my business," "this cannot be").

**23.3. Speech Markers**

Markers of fragmentation in speech: tempo (acceleration without foundation, ragged rhythm); pauses (absence of a pause before an answer); intonation (raising at the end of a statement, interrogative intonation in a statement); fillers ("uh," "well," "like," "in general" in the absence of content); self-interruption (beginning a phrase without completing it).

Markers of pressure in speech: volume (raising in the absence of external noise); rhythm (acceleration upon transition to instructions); formulations ("must," "should," "obliged" in the absence of foundation).

Markers of coherence in speech: even tempo; pause before an answer; complete sentences; absence of contradictions; precise formulations.

**23.4. Visual Markers**

Markers of tension: jaw clenched; shoulders raised, slouched; posture compressed, closed; breathing shallow, in the chest; hands clenched, hidden. Markers of relaxation: jaw relaxed; shoulders lowered, turned out; posture open; breathing deep, in the belly; hands free. Position: visual markers are captured by video recording. Accuracy depends on the quality of the recording and lighting. The result is a probabilistic assessment, not a diagnosis.

**23.5. Mechanism of Processing**

The machine performs three operations.

**First — detection.** Markers are extracted from the stream: textual, speech, visual. Each marker is entered as a fact: "contradiction detected," "raising of tone detected," "clenching of jaw detected."

**Second — comparison.** Markers are compared with archetype models. Observed position: a combination of markers indicates a probable archetype. For example, the combination "imperative mood + raising of volume + clenching of jaw" indicates the Eggplant with a probability above threshold.

**Third — assessment.** The result of recognition is transmitted to the human in the form of a probabilistic assessment: "probability of pressure — 70%," "probability of fragmentation — 60%." The assessment is a basis for verification, not a verdict.

**23.6. Limitations**

Limitations of machine recognition: 1) probabilistic character (the result is an assessment, not a fact; accuracy depends on the model, data, and context); 2) context (the same marker in different contexts has different meaning; raising of volume under external noise — adaptation, under silence — a marker of pressure); 3) cultural differences (markers of tension and relaxation differ in different cultures; the model requires calibration); 4) consent (recognition is performed with the consent of the observed; without consent observation changes the conditions of interaction).

**23.7. Application**

Position: machine recognition is applied as an auxiliary instrument in the selection of candidates for positions with a high cost of error, in negotiations, in assessing the state of the environment before making a decision. Order: the machine detects markers — the human interprets the result — the decision is made by the human. The final decision remains with the human; the machine performs detection and preliminary analysis.

**23.8. Connection with the ICM Model**

Machine recognition relies on the position of ICM: coherence manifests in form. Form is captured instrumentally and observationally. The machine performs the detection of form; the assessment of state is performed by the aggregate of markers. Consequence: coherence becomes a measurable parameter in real time. This opens the possibility of application in environments where a decision is made quickly and the cost of error is high.
