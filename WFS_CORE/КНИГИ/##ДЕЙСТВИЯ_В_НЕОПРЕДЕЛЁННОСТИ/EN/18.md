**Chapter 12. Three Doors**

**12.1. The Corridor with Three Doors**

A known image: a corridor, long, dim. At the end — three doors: one on the left, one straight ahead, one on the right. Behind each door is a decision. The door leads to a result when conditions coincide; when there is no coincidence, behind the door is found emptiness, a wall, or a mirror.

The left door corresponds to the wrong time. On opening, emptiness is seen: the opportunity has already left or has not yet come. Entry spends forces on an action that cannot work. Basis — the moment is wrong.

The right door corresponds to the wrong place. After opening it, the subject runs into a wall, a cliff, or falls into a swamp. Entry leads to loss of resource. Basis — the dislocation is wrong.

The middle door corresponds to the wrong state. On opening, a mirror is found: the reflection of a tired, frightened, hurried person. Entry is not performed. Basis — the decision-maker is not ready.

The coincidence of all three — time, place, and state — opens the door to the needed decision. Not to a decision in general, but to this decision, at this moment, in this place, with this state. Position: this is the assembly point, where the decision is born by itself, and forces are not spent on what cannot work.

**12.2. Desha — Place**

Desha is defined as a space of opportunities, not a geographic point and not an address. Place strengthens or depletes. One place nourishes: on entry, lightness of breathing, clarity of thoughts, assembly of decisions are noted. Basis — the environment does not resist. Another place depletes: on entry, constraint, heaviness, murkiness, effort at every word are noted. Basis — the environment is hostile.

Desha is determined by the criterion "where it works," not "where it is convenient." This is the place where the verdict comes by itself, a small effort gives a tangible result, and efficiency reaches 85%, not 12.5%.

**Model situation.** A leader receives a promising offer in another city, in another culture, in another environment. They agree on the basis of benefit. A year later burnout occurs. Basis — the place is wrong: the environment took the energy. The decision did not work because desha was wrong.

**12.3. Kala — Time**

Kala is defined as the quality of the moment, not the clock on the wall and not the date in the calendar. Kala indicates the point where action hits or misses.

Two states are distinguished: time to act and time to wait. Waiting manifests as accumulation, preparation, strengthening of stability. A seed under the ground does not sleep; it grows invisibly. Extraction before the term leads to destruction. Basis — not the time.

The error of the majority consists in ignoring windows: action is performed when one must wait, and waiting is preserved when one must act. Basis — the quality of the moment is not taken into account. Attention is directed at the clock; the clock shows chronology, kala reflects quality.

**Model situation.** A company is ready for launch: everything is calculated, everything is ready. The market has not yet matured. The launch fails. Basis — kala is wrong. A year later another company launches the same product and achieves success. Basis — hitting the window. Kala worked.

**12.4. Patra — State**

Patra is defined as the state inside, not circumstances outside. This is the readiness of the decision-maker, the level of integrity, the ability to carry a decision.

A person may be in the right place and at the right time while remaining unready: fatigue, fright, fragmentation. Entry is not performed. Basis — the person themselves is wrong.

Patra is defined as a measurable state by metrics: #IH, #FI, #SEI, #CLARITY. Readiness criteria: #IH ≥ 6, #FI ≤ 5, #CLARITY ≥ 9. When indicators decrease, unreadiness is established.

**Model situation.** A general receives an order. Time and place correspond. He has not slept for three days: #IH — 4, #FI — 6. The order is given. The decision fails. Basis — patra is wrong: fatigue distorted perception.

**12.5. Assembly Point**

The assembly point is the moment of coincidence of all three factors: desha > 7, kala > 7, patra > 7. Values are accepted as sufficient, not ideal. At this point action is performed now, without shifting earlier or later.

The assembly point is determined in #SUSLIK-#SAGE mode: "Here desha works. Now kala is open. The state patra is ready." Only after this is the step performed.

When the value of one of the factors is below 6, a mode of waiting-preparation is established. It is not paralysis. Desha < 6 — change of place. Kala < 6 — transfer of action. Patra < 6 — restoration of state. Action is performed after coincidence.

**12.6. Checklist for the Decision-Maker**

Five questions, thirty seconds, before every decision.

**First question:** Does the decision relate to the zone of responsibility of the one making it? With a negative answer the decision is not made: a transfer is formalized, without control and worry.

**Second:** Does the moment correspond to the quality of kala? Is the window open or closed? With a closed window, waiting and preparation are established, without forcing the event.

**Third:** Does the place strengthen the decision or deplete it? Under depletion, a change of dislocation is performed, without patience.

**Fourth:** Is the decision-maker ready? #IH > 6, #FI < 5, #CLARITY > 9? Under non-correspondence, restoration through state is appointed, without effort through force.

**Fifth:** Is there #BI? Fact or interpretation? Under interpretation, #CLARIFICATION is performed: a request, not an assumption.

Five questions cut off 80% of false actions, before the verdict is made, not after.

**12.7. What This Gives**

The three doors appear as a map: where time is, where place is, where state is, and where they coincide. Coincidence occurs in the current moment, not forever. With a visible map, the decision does not require a feat: it fits like a key into a lock. Basis — precision, coincidence, clarity.

The door opens when all three coincide: time, place, and state. The decision is perceived as natural, light, precise, obvious.
