**Appendix A. Practical Part. Mirror Before Decision**

**Introduction**

Below are questions for self-observation. There is no evaluation. Each answer is entered as a fact that can be checked: observation of one's own behavior over the last thirty days. The content of the answers remains with the one answering. The purpose of the tests is to see the current state from which decisions are made. Method: the answer is entered as a whole number from 0 to 10. Then the three numbers are added and compared with 10, as in the metrics #IH, #FI, and #INF. A discrepancy indicates a zone where the assessment requires verification.

**Test 1. Gratitude**

**Questions. Each answer — a number from 0 to 10.**

1. How often is another person's contribution — a subordinate's, a colleague's, a partner's — noticed at the moment of receiving help?
2. How often is this contribution spoken aloud?
3. How often is a contribution noticed that is not visible in reports: advice, a warning, a task removed from the leader?

**What the answer means.**

- 0–2: the contribution of others is rarely noticed. Attention is directed at the result. The contribution to the result is invisible because it does not enter the field of view.
- 3–5: the contribution is tracked selectively. Depends on circumstances.
- 6–8: the contribution is tracked regularly. A connection is observed between attention to contribution and the quality of interaction.
- 9–10: the contribution is noticed at the moment of receipt. A connection is observed between attention and state.

**Discharge.** A person who is not told "thank you" stops doing, after a month, that for which they are not thanked. They remain in place, but the action disappears. A leader who does not notice contribution, after a month, manages a team that is present and does not act. This is not morality. This is observation.

**Connection with economics.** The effect of gratitude is a transition to parasympathetic dominance. Parasympathetic is connected with regeneration. Regeneration is connected with the quality of decisions. In the absence of attention to contribution, the environment shifts into defense mode, and the resource leaves the task for defensive reactions. The economics is reflected in #ISK_DEV and #AC.

**Test 2. Apology**

**Questions. Each answer — a number from 0 to 10.**

1. How often, upon discovering one's own error that affected another, is the error acknowledged aloud?
2. How often is the acknowledgment performed without explanations, without references to circumstances, without pointing to another's actions?
3. How often does a change of action follow the acknowledgment, and not only a word?

**What the answer means.**

- 0–2: the error is rarely acknowledged. It is described as an external circumstance. The environment receives a signal: the error is not noticed.
- 3–5: the error is acknowledged selectively. The acknowledgment depends on the price and the audience.
- 6–8: the error is acknowledged regularly. A connection is observed between acknowledgment and the speed of correction.
- 9–10: the error is acknowledged at the moment of detection. A connection is observed between acknowledgment and the stability of the environment.

**Discharge.** An apology followed by an explanation is an apology and an explanation in one package. The listener hears the explanation. The apology is lost along the way. The formulation "I am sorry, but" is perceived by the receiving side as "but." Further content is not retained.

**Connection with economics.** Acknowledgment of an error interrupts the cascade: the error does not pass into concealment, concealment — into repetition, repetition — into systemic failure. The cascade is described in the chapter on the structure of failure. Economics: one unacknowledged error at the stage of occurrence costs more at the stage of consequences.

**Test 3. Noticing a Contribution That Is Not Visible**

**Questions. Each answer — a number from 0 to 10.**

1. How often is a contribution noticed that is not reflected in reports: a warning about risk, a removed task, a team kept from error?
2. How often is the contribution noticed at the moment of receipt?
3. How often does attention to the contribution manifest in the presence of the one whose contribution is noted?

**What the answer means.**

- 0–2: contribution outside reports is invisible. Visibility is determined by reporting.
- 3–5: contribution outside reports is tracked selectively.
- 6–8: contribution outside reports is tracked regularly.
- 9–10: contribution is noticed at the moment of receipt and in presence.

**Discharge.** Contribution outside reports has the property of disappearing from memory by the next meeting. In a month the leader remembers the result and does not remember who produced it. In a year the producer of the contribution does not remember why they produced it. Observed consequence: work is performed, attention is absent, the connection between work and result is broken.

**Connection with economics.** An unnoticed contribution is not reproduced. Economics: repeated search for the executor, repeated training, loss of accumulated context.

**Test 4. State at the Moment of Decision**

**Questions. Each answer — a number from 0 to 10.**

1. How often before a decision is the state tracked: tension in the body, rate of breathing, position of the hands?
2. How often is the decision postponed for the time necessary to reduce tension?
3. How often is the decision made in a state in which relaxation is noted?

**What the answer means.**

- 0–2: the state before a decision is not tracked. The decision is made in the current state.
- 3–5: the state is noticed when there is time.
- 6–8: the state is tracked regularly.
- 9–10: the state is noticed at the moment of decision-making.

**Discharge.** A decision made in tension seems erroneous an hour later. A decision made in relaxation seems obvious an hour later. The difference is in the state from which the decision was made. The content of the decision in both cases may coincide. Consequence: what seems erroneous is not the decision but the state in which it was made.

**Connection with economics.** The state is measured instrumentally: HRV, cortisol, tension index. A decision from a state of high coherence converges with facts. A decision from a state of low coherence requires rework. Rework is reflected in #ISK_DEV. Economics: the cost of one rework equals the cost of ten decisions made from clarity.

**Summary. Verification**

Four tests, three questions in each. Twelve answers. The sum for each test is compared with 30 — the maximum value. Observed position: a discrepancy between answers indicates a zone in which self-observation requires verification. For example, the answer "gratitude — 9" with the answer "attention to contribution outside reports — 2" indicates a difference between intention and action. The answer "apology — 9" with the answer "change of action — 2" indicates a difference between word and deed. Noting the discrepancy is not an evaluation. It is observation, from which it is visible where attention is in noise and where it is in reality. The state from which the decision is made is determined by the aggregate of answers.

**For Those Who Read and Do Not Take the Tests**

The tests are not mandatory. Observed position: a leader who does not track state before a decision makes a decision in a state they do not notice. In a month they remember the result and do not remember the state. In a year they remember the results and do not remember which of them were made from clarity and which from tension. The connection between state and result is broken. Tracking this connection is the subject of the tests.
