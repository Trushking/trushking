**Chapter 25. How to Recognize a Coherent Subject**

**25.1. Statement**

A coherent subject does not differ externally from those around them. There is no glow, no miracles, no unusual effects. Speech — ordinary. Clothing — ordinary. Status — ordinary or absent. From this follows a practical question: by what signs can a fragmented observer identify a coherent subject. The signs must be accessible to perception without instruments and without preparation.

**25.2. First Sign: Speech**

In the speech of a coherent subject, complete sentences and precise formulations are traced. There are no contradictions within a single message. Unnecessary clarifications and repetitions are not found. The tempo is even, before an answer — a pause. Defensive formulations ("possibly," "probably," "I am not sure") are not applied. The listener involuntarily notes the absence of threat markers; their #FI decreases. They remain in observation mode.

**25.3. Second Sign: Reaction to Error**

An error is acknowledged at the moment of detection. Form: "error," "I am correcting." Without explanations, without references to circumstances, without pointing to another's actions. The cascade is interrupted at the first link: the error does not pass into concealment, concealment — into repetition.

**25.4. Third Sign: Fixation of Contribution**

Another's contribution is noted at the moment of receipt. Form: "I thank you," "this is your contribution." Without exaggeration, without understatement. The receiver fixes the acknowledgment. The environment receives a signal: the contribution is visible. The reproduction of the contribution is preserved.

**25.5. Fourth Sign: Vision of Losses**

Losses in the system are noticed even in the area where the subject is not a specialist. Formulations: "time is lost here," "the decision does not pass here," "the signal is blocked here." Mechanism: losses are noted as a deviation from reality. Special knowledge for detecting the deviation is not required. Clarity of perception is required. Advice is given on the substance, without details, accessible to verification.

**25.6. Fifth Sign: Absence of Status Game**

References to regalia, pressure by position, demand for recognition are absent. Formulations: "this can be checked," "the source is here." The observer sees the absence of pressure. Pressure is a marker of low coherence; its absence is a marker of high.

**25.7. Sixth Sign: State of the Environment**

In interaction with a coherent subject the state of those around changes. A decrease in tension, a slowing of tempo, pauses, precision of formulations are noted. Mechanism: biobehavioral synchronism (ICM). Transmission is performed through form. The environment acquires a supporting structure. This is noted as a change of state, not as an impression.

**25.8. Seventh Sign: Verifiability**

Statements are verifiable. Formulations: "check," "source," "fact." Upon checking, statements coincide with facts. A divergence is noted as an error and acknowledged.

**25.9. Practical Consequence**

A coherent subject is recognized by the aggregate of signs, not by one. A single sign can be reproduced by a fragmented subject. The aggregate — is reproduced rarely. For the observer: the identification of signs is performed during interaction. Special preparation is not required. Attention to form is required: speech, reaction to error, fixation of contribution, absence of pressure, verifiability. For the leader: in selecting candidates for positions with a high cost of error, the aggregate of signs is the basis for verification. Regalia, status, recommendations — additional data; the aggregate of signs — primary.
