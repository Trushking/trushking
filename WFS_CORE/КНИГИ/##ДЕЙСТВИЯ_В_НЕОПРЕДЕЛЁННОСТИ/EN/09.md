The heart has its own neural network and bidirectional connection with the brain. The neural traffic from the heart to the brain exceeds the reverse. The heart receives commands and forms a signal that the brain processes.

The state designated in the literature as physiological coherence, or heart rate coherence, is measured through heart rate variability (HRV). In this state the rhythm pattern becomes ordered, approaching sinusoidal, with a peak around 0.1 Hz, which corresponds to approximately 10 seconds per cycle.

The key physiological correlate is a shift of autonomic balance toward strengthening parasympathetic activity. Additionally, synchronization between the two branches of the autonomic nervous system, increased resonance of the vascular system, and synchronization of brain alpha rhythms with the heart rhythm are noted.
