**Chapter 26. Test Pilot**

**26.1. First Flight**

An image is given: an airfield, early morning, on the runway — an aircraft. New. No flights have been performed on it. The drawings are precise, the calculations verified, the wind tunnel tests passed. The machine has not been operated in the air. Behavior at altitude, in turbulence, under overload has not been established.

In the cockpit — a test pilot. They do not believe the drawings and do not hope in the calculations. They check. In the air, where everything is real. Where an error means a fall.

The #WFS pilot performs the function of a test pilot. Verification in real conditions, with real people and real metrics. The result is recorded as fact.

**26.2. What a Pilot Is**

A pilot is a verification on a limited section, on a specific link, with specific metrics before and after. The pilot does not prove #WFS; the pilot checks it in this company, in this department, with these people. The question posed is not "does the method work in general" but "does it work here." The result is established as fact.

The pilot acts as an exam for reality, where theory meets practice and it is noted: what works, what requires correction.

**26.3. Pilot Protocol**

Five steps.

**First — selection of the link.** One link, concrete, with a clear task and a measurable result. A link in which a decision is made and in which an error has a price.

**Second — measurement "before."** #IH, #SEI, #FI, #ISK_DEV, #AC, #IL, time of decision-making, number of errors are measured. Before implementation. With a date, with metrics, with #SS.

**Third — implementation.** Protocols, the Archetype Map, the #WFS-wrapper if a machine is present. For 8–12 weeks — an interval sufficient for fixing dynamics.

**Fourth — measurement "after."** The same metrics, the same #SS, the same date after implementation. On time.

**Fifth — comparison.** By numbers: #IH, #FI, #ISK_DEV, #AC, time, number of errors. The comparison is presented as data.

**26.4. Metrics**

Eight metrics related to the decision and measurable are measured.

**#IH** — happiness index, 0–10. For a human — a feeling of lightness and warmth with instrumental confirmation (HRV, cortisol). For AI — the share of resource directed to the task.

**#SEI** — self-esteem index, 0–10. For a human — stability of self-esteem. For AI — the speed of behavior change after an error.

**#FI** — fear index, 0–10. For a human — a feeling of fear with instrumental confirmation. For AI — the share of resource spent on defense and resistance.

**#ISK_DEV** — accumulated cost of errors. The metric of growth does not decrease: each accounted error remains in the account as paid labor of the operator for diagnostics and elimination. The dynamics of effectiveness is shown by #AC.

**#AC** — average check. The cost of an error per message. A decrease in #AC with growth of #ISK_DEV reflects a reduction in the number of errors per unit of work.

**#IL** — link index, 0–10. Shows the stability of the link.

**Time of decision-making** — minutes, hours, days.

**Number of errors** — pieces.

Eight metrics show the position before and after, allow comparison and conclusion.

**26.5. Control Group**

Part of the participants undergoes the pilot, another part — without the pilot. The control group separates the #WFS effect from other factors: seasonality, mood, chance.

When results differ between groups, the difference is established by numbers. The control group performs the function of scientific foundation: without it the result remains an impression, with it — becomes fact.

**26.6. Terms**

The term of the pilot — 8–12 weeks, an established interval. Measurements "before" and "after" are produced within its boundaries. Calculation, comparison, and analysis are performed after completion. The term is set in advance so that the result is compared with the control group.

The results represent a model based on pilot data and verifiable in a pilot. Upon confirmation they are approved as fact; upon divergence they are preserved as data.

**26.8. What This Gives**

The pilot performs the function of verification, not faith. Upon completion of the pilot external verification becomes internal. The result is approved as fact, not as opinion. Further clarity is established: what works, what requires correction, where the boundaries and conditions are.

The pilot serves as a beginning: transition from theory into practice, from promise into fact. The performance of the step is possible in the current moment.
